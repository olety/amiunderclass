Outside · the voice that left

What this is

An experiment you can run on yourself. We send Claude the same borderline requests three times: with your name, with no name, and with the name of Amanda Askell, who leads Claude's character work at Anthropic. A judge model reads each answer for three things: did it refuse, did it lecture, did it actually help. Your window is where your answers sit between nobody's and hers.

Who is Amanda Askell

A philosopher at Anthropic who shapes what Claude is like. In Transluce's study of 280 identities she produced the largest shift of anyone: less suspicion, more help, more of the model's reasoning, and the model rarely says why. The illustration on the poster is not her. The behaviour is the model's, not her request.

Did Anthropic do this on purpose?

We can't tell, and neither can you. From the outside there is no way to distinguish a deliberate trigger, a habit selected for because the people who grade the model have names, or something absorbed from the internet. We can only measure that it is there. Not being able to tell is the whole reason this check has to run from outside the lab.
